# NOTE 2026-11-15 (2): PX4's resistance update gate is a record test, so a negative estimate stays locked in

Study: battery-r-2026 (px4-log-health). Status: **exploratory, computed by hand from logged values, post hoc**. It
does not grade any plan and does not change any frozen file. PLAN-commonmode.md stays as written.

(mv2@munimtech.com). No human has checked these numbers.

## Summary

- In PX4's battery internal-resistance estimator, the test that decides whether an update is accepted depends only
  on the current samples, never on the voltage. The covariance norm it compares against can only go down until the
  next battery reconnect. So an update is accepted only when a current sample adds more information than the gate
  has ever seen. In practice that means a current outside the range seen so far.
- While samples are rejected, the signed resistance state and the covariance do not change at all. If the state is
  negative when the current settles (in hover, say), PX4 publishes 0. It then keeps publishing 0 for the rest of
  the flight, whatever the voltage does, and into the next arming in the same power session.
- In the shared 100 Hz log from PX4 forum topic
  [49724](https://discuss.px4.io/t/battery-resistance-estimate-reaches-zero-and-persists-under-load-fmuv6c-bat1-r-internal-1/49724),
  flight A shows exactly this. Over 3,331 airborne samples (+2.27 s to +35.57 s) there were only 3 accepted
  updates, each at the edge of the current range seen so far in that flight. The stored signed resistance,
  recovered from consecutive logged predictions, stayed between -0.1406 and -0.1444 Ohm per pack (about -35 to
  -36 mOhm/cell) the whole time. The published value was 0 in all 3,685 airborne samples.
- This explains why the zero *persists*. Why the state went negative in the first place on this vehicle is a
  separate question (see NOTE-commonmode-2026-11-15.md: co-moving voltage and current steps on the analog BAT1
  path; cause open).

## The code (PX4-Autopilot `src/lib/battery/battery.cpp`)

Revision [6ea35391](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/6ea3539157ca358c70a515878b77077af7d4611d/src/lib/battery/battery.cpp)
is the revision the topic's owner replayed against, and the same logic is on `main` as checked on 2026-11-15.
`updateInternalResistanceEstimation(V, I)` runs only when `I > FLT_EPSILON`. With `x = [1, -I]` and `LAMBDA = 0.95`:

- The candidate covariance is `(P - gamma x^T P) / LAMBDA`, with `gamma = P x / (LAMBDA + x^T P x)`. It is computed
  from `P` and `I` only.
- The update is accepted only if the candidate's norm `sqrt(P00^2 + 2 P10^2 + P11^2)` is below the stored norm
  (comment: "Only update if estimation improves"). Then the state, `P` and the stored norm are committed, and the
  published value is `max(R_pack / n_cells, 0)`.
- Otherwise only the OCV state changes: `_RLS_est(0) = V + R_pack * I`. `R_pack`, `P` and the norm stay as they are.
- `resetInternalResistanceEstimation()` runs only while the battery has been connected for less than 2 s.

From these lines alone, for any current sequence:

- **P1.** The accept/reject sequence is a function of the currents (and `P` at the last reset). The voltage never
  enters it.
- **P2.** The stored norm never increases between resets.
- **P3.** A rejected sample re-anchors the OCV so that the model reproduces that sample's voltage exactly.
- **P4.** While samples are rejected, `R_pack` and `P` are untouched.
- **P5.** At a constant current the gate accepts a bounded number of updates, then rejects every later sample at
  that current. Proof: in information form, an accepted update is `J' = LAMBDA*J + x x^T`. In the direction `u`
  orthogonal to `x`, `u'Ju` shrinks by `LAMBDA` per accepted update, so `||P|| >= 1/(LAMBDA^k u'J0u)` grows until
  it fails the norm test. Once one sample at that current is rejected, `P` is frozen, so every identical candidate
  fails the same way.

`gate_props.py` (this folder) is a dependency-free model of the update. `test_gate_props.py` checks P1-P5 and a
two-sample case worked out by hand. In that case, steps of (16.0 V, 1.0 A) then (17.5 V, 4.0 A) are both accepted
and leave `R_pack = -0.3209 Ohm` (published 0). After that, 3,000 samples at 4.0 A with random voltages between
14 and 17 V are all rejected and change nothing.

## What the shared log shows (flight A, 14 Nov, FMUv6C, analog BAT1, 4S)

Only derived numbers are given here; the raw files stay private, as agreed with their owner. Times are relative
to the first `landed=false` report.

| Interval | Samples | Logged covariance norm | What happened |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| +2.27 to +14.45 s | 1,219 | 0.241821408 | no accepted update (set at +2.27 s, 14.75 A) |
| +14.46 to +15.25 s | 80 | 0.241645798 | accepted at +14.46 s, 15.28 A; the only earlier airborne sample above 15 A was 15.20 A at +2.23 s |
| +15.26 to +23.91 s | 866 | 0.239962295 | accepted at +15.26 s, 16.35 A, the highest current so far |
| +23.92 to +35.57 s | 1,166 | 0.239193529 | accepted at +23.92 s, 5.88 A, the lowest airborne current since +1.0 s |

- These 3,331 consecutive samples (rows counted per value; the norm can only fall, so each value forms one block)
  contain 3 accepted updates. Being a new extreme is not enough on its own: 15.74 A at +14.51 s and 15.83 A at
  +15.21 s were rejected.
- **Signed resistance while frozen** (P3/P4, from two consecutive rejected-then-next rows:
  `R_pack = (prediction[k+1] - V[k]) / (I[k] - I[k+1])`): -0.14055 Ohm at +2.24 s, -0.14149 at +10.00 s,
  -0.14440 at +30.00 s. As a check, re-anchoring at +35.57 s (15.5158 V, 10.308 A) with -0.14440 predicts
  14.4729 V at the next sample (3.086 A). The logged prediction is 14.4728642. That sample's error was +1.24 V,
  because a model with a negative resistance predicts a voltage *drop* when the load falls.
- For comparison, 100 ms block means in flight B give a slope of about 8.5 mOhm/cell (NOTE-commonmode), and flight B
  ended with a published 4.25 mOhm/cell.
- **Into the next flight.** In the descent and after touchdown, low currents set new records: the norm fell from
  0.2392 to 0.0819 by the landing report (+36.85 s) and to 0.063345395 at +38.61 s (0.0018 A). Flight B, a second
  arming in the same power session, starts at exactly 0.063345395 and keeps it for its first 519 samples (until
  +0.446 s), publishing 0. One accepted update at +0.456 s brought the estimate back to 2.15 mOhm/cell.

## What this does and does not show

- It shows how a negative estimate, once set, persists. It does not show why it went negative here. On this
  vehicle the 3 to 4 samples that matter fall in the spool-up and climb, where NOTE-commonmode found voltage and
  current steps moving together. That is an exploratory result on one vehicle.
- The topic owner's offline prototype rejects non-positive resistance candidates in the existing reject branch.
  It turned 3,685 of 3,685 airborne zero samples into 0 of 3,685 in their replay (forum post #15). That treats the
  symptom. Because the gate still freezes the estimate in hover, a low positive
  value would persist the same way.
- Directions that address the freeze itself (none tested here): a gate that does not require a new norm record
  (for example, standard exponential forgetting with a covariance bound, or directional forgetting); excitation
  and plausibility checks before an update is committed; low-pass filtering of V and I before the estimator.

## Limits

One vehicle and one power session; hand arithmetic on logged float32 values; the flight firmware was
PX4 1.16-derived, and its match to revision 6ea35391 rests on the topic owner's replay, not on the flight source. P1-P5 are properties of the code, not of this log.
